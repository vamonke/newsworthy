# Newsworthy design brief

## Design read

Newsworthy is a fast social game for players who enjoy creating absurd breaking-news moments. The interface should feel like a local TV field desk under deadline: useful first, heightened second, and playful without becoming a parody dashboard.

## Design dials

- `DESIGN_VARIANCE: 8` - asymmetric opening composition and strong broadcast blocks
- `MOTION_INTENSITY: 7` - purposeful entrances, capture feedback, and live-state motion
- `VISUAL_DENSITY: 6` - enough newsroom information to create urgency without covering the feed

## Preserve

- The two-color Newsworthy wordmark
- The live camera as the dominant surface
- Scene creation, custom prompts, event history, and camera roll
- The lower third, ticker, photo sale, and round recap
- Existing IDs and control names used by game logic
- Keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, and explicit game states

## Retire

- The centered instruction card that feels separate from the game world
- Large blocks of undifferentiated blue controls
- Weak hierarchy between primary play actions and secondary information
- The empty camera-roll emoji
- Flat panels with no sense of newsroom equipment or physical media

## Direction

Use a cold newsroom palette with signal red as the only accent and amber only for capture readiness. Pair condensed broadcast type with a practical sans-serif. Treat panels as equipment bays and contact sheets, using square corners and deliberate hairlines. Motion must communicate a state change: going live, receiving an assignment, capturing a photo, or receiving the editor's decision.

The opening image is a commissioned helicopter-news still showing a giant rubber duck, a flying saucer, and smoke above a dense city. It establishes the game in one glance and gives the opening dialog a real visual anchor.
